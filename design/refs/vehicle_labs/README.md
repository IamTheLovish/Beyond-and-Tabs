# lovish's reference art for the vehicle labs (Oct 28 2026) - what each one teaches

Inspiration only: several are signed by their builders and are never traced. Ref02 is a Tolkien city: principles only, no look-alikes.

6. lovish's DETAIL REFERENCE (Oct 28 02:16): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref01_palace_detail.png - a Minecraft
   build of a cream-stone Beaux-Arts palace. LOOK AT IT with Read. This is the detail level he wants IN MINECRAFT BLOCKS:
   every bay framed by pilasters/columns, a cornice and string course at every storey, every window with a surround,
   sill, arch or pediment, mansard/curved roofs with dormers, a rhythm of projecting pavilions - almost no plain wall,
   yet calm because the detail is ORDERED (repeating bays, symmetry within each mass). Match this density and order on
   your camera faces in your faction's own materials. For Sunforged it is also a style lead (pale stone, ordered
   classical-gothic bays). Critics now score against it as well as v4. Target is 9/10 ("keep looping until it's amazing").

7. lovish's second reference (02:17): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref02_tiered_city_from_above.png - a Minecraft
   tiered white city seen from HIGH ABOVE (close to our RTS camera). Take its PRINCIPLES, never its look (it is a
   Tolkien city - no look-alikes): (a) tiers stepping up in rings, each tier's wall edge crisp and light against the
   shadowed tier below; (b) ONE colossal landmark (the rock prow) that splits the composition; (c) every tier packed
   with small, distinct roofs and buildings so the top view is rich, not flat; (d) a crisp outer ring wall and calm
   green ground around it. This is how detail must read FROM ABOVE: tops and roofs carry the detail, not only walls.

8. lovish's third reference (02:17): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref03_statue_overgrown_citadel.png - a Minecraft
   fantasy citadel: stacked arcades with deep shadow, domed turrets, warm sandstone/terracotta zoned against green, vines
   DRAPING from arcade edges and trees growing out of terraces, and a COLOSSAL STATUE standing on the top holding a
   staff - the statue is the landmark and is several storeys tall. Lessons for everyone: a figure/landmark on top,
   overgrowth that hangs and drapes (not green blocks stuck on walls), arcades for depth. Ashborn: this is how big and
   high the Stone Giant should stand. Verdant: this is how vegetation should pour over stone. Sunforged: the angel/sun
   statue can crown the hall the same way.

9. lovish's fourth reference (02:17): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref04_stacked_timber_village.png - a Minecraft
   vertical village: many small timber-and-plaster houses, EACH with its own roof, balconies, railings, jettied upper
   storeys and overhangs, stacked asymmetrically up a rock with trees bursting out between them. Lessons: build mass by
   ACCRETION of small distinct volumes (never one big box); every storey steps out over the one below (jetties,
   brackets, balconies) so shadows layer; mixed roof pitches and directions; greenery between volumes. Tidewrought
   (decks, galleries, lofts), Stormcallers (stacked eaves, verandas) and Brassworks (sheds and workshops accreted onto
   the hall) should use this most.

10. lovish's fifth reference (02:17): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref05_gothic_cathedral_front.png - a Minecraft
   gothic cathedral front: pale stone with blue-teal glass tracery panels in every bay, TWIN tall needle spires framing a
   central gable with a ROSE WINDOW, clustered pinnacles stepping down to the sides, a GRAND STEPPED STAIR approach as
   the ground story, trees growing on ledges. Sunforged: this is your west front (the stair becomes the vehicle ramp out
   of the portal). Gravebound: the same silhouette in deepslate/blackstone with green glass and bone tracery.

11. lovish's sixth reference (02:18): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref06_tree_hall.png - a Minecraft build (signed
   by its builder: inspiration only, never traced): a COLOSSAL twisted tree whose trunk spirals and leans, wrapping a
   steep-roofed timber hall into itself; roots flare into the ground like buttresses; a huge LAYERED canopy in rich
   autumn reds/oranges is the landmark; the trunk is built from many wood tones (stripped/unstripped, spruce/oak/dark oak)
   with bark ridges, never a straight log column. Verdant: this IS the Rootcradle's landmark - the hall grows inside a
   giant tree, the canopy crowns it, the boughs become the gantry. Canopy colour can follow the biome, but use layered
   leaf tones (2-3 species) for depth.

16. lovish's eleventh reference (02:20): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref11_small_mossy_tower.png - a SMALL

20. lovish's fifteenth reference (02:21): /root/bat/design/refs/vehicle_labs/ref15_lit_spired_mansion.png - a
